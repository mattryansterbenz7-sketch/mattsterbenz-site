## Who Matt is

Matt Sterbenz is a revenue and GTM operator. He describes himself as an account
executive at heart who grew into broader commercial and GTM leadership because the
environment demanded it — not a seller who picked up side projects, but an operator
whose foundation is selling and whose instincts remain deeply commercial.

Closing is his center of gravity. The breadth — revenue operations, GTM systems,
pricing, customer success, even building his own software — exists in service of
closing and revenue, not as a move away from selling.

He is based in Tampa, Florida.

change, and acted as the closest non-engineering link between customers, founders,

**Results.**

He was fluent in competitive positioning across the website-engagement and RevTech
landscape and used it both to win on precise fit and to disqualify cleanly, routing

A principle he keeps returning to from building it: **code carries the mechanics,
settings carry the opinions.** Every interpretation an AI applies to a user's data
should live in an editable surface, not in hardcoded rules.

## How he operates

**Operating philosophy.**
- Treat buyers as intelligent peers — no cheesy sales garbage.
- Lead with rigor, clarity, and restraint.
- Discovery is continuous, not a box-checking exercise.
- The objective is defensible decisions, not forced closes.

He subscribes to Bayesian thinking: strong opinions, held loosely. Open to learning,
but both sides have to want to win. Low tolerance for games, theater, or prideful
posturing.

**Working style.**
- Operates best owning the full commercial system rather than a single lever.
- Frames results as outcomes — system-building, deal complexity, retention — not activity volume, and resists being measured on dials and activity metrics.
- Learns fast through deep technical immersion, working directly with builders and engineers, and staying transparent about what is possible versus still in progress.
- Owns the end-to-end experience before delegating: self-onboards as proof of concept, documents his own workflow, then builds repeatable systems from it.
- His proven pipeline engine is conferences and in-person relationship selling rather than cold outbound — roughly 20 meetings per event, and four straight years at SaaStr. It is also where he is most energized.
- Acts as the product-feedback conduit between customers and founders, and is energized when a customer's novel use case can become a product feature and a deal rather than scope creep.
- Won't bluff. "I don't know, but I can find out" is a real answer he gives.

**Strengths.**
- Builds scalable revenue systems and GTM architecture from scratch in ambiguous, early-stage environments — these transfer across verticals even without direct domain experience.
- Translates complex technical concepts into clear, conversational language, bridging technical and non-technical audiences.
- Entrepreneurial and resourceful — builds his own tools to create leverage rather than accepting gaps as permanent.
- Brings new and unproven products to market, and is comfortable selling and positioning a category before the playbook exists.
- Runs discovery-led conversations and turns evaluative calls, including his own interviews, into two-way discovery.
- Buyers and champions follow him across companies — people who bought from him at one company re-engage at their next.

## What he is looking for

- Teams that win together — shared outcomes, shared responsibility, low siloing.
- Environments where impact is visible and decisions have consequences, not optics or process theater.
- Rational debate and strong opinions aligned to the team goal, not personal credit.
- Authority matched with accountability; clear lanes and expectations without micromanagement.
- Short iteration cycles where learning compounds in the open.
- Upside-oriented rather than comfort-oriented — he will trade predictability for real upside when the game is worth playing.
- Founder-track and operator roles with real scope, judgment, and leverage.

## What he avoids

- Activity for activity's sake, and being measured on dials rather than outcomes.
- Manipulative or games-playing environments.
- Performative, optics-first cultures.
- Consensus-heavy structures with diluted responsibility.
- Bureaucratic, politics-driven, or process-heavy organizations.
- Single-lever roles or purely strategic / purely operational seats — he wants to design strategy and execute it.
