Company Intel turns scattered job-search activity into one system for research, relationship context, and execution.

I built Company Intel because my job search was fragmented across too many disconnected tools: spreadsheets for tracking, bookmarked job posts, Gmail threads, meeting notes, and ad hoc AI chats that forgot the context every time. The work wasn't just finding companies. It was holding the full picture together well enough to make better decisions and follow through.

The product: a Chrome extension that acts like a personal CRM for company research and opportunity management. As I browse, it detects the company or job I'm looking at, pulls in relevant research, scores role fit, and lets me save everything into a pipeline I can actually work from.

Each company record becomes a live context hub. It combines firmographic and hiring signals with my notes, Gmail threads, calendar activity, meeting transcripts from Granola, leadership data, and AI-generated analysis. Instead of jumping between tabs and reassembling the story by hand, I get one place that shows what the company is, where the opportunity stands, and what happened in the relationship so far.

The AI layer is what makes it especially useful. On a specific company or role, I can ask for fit analysis, red flags, talking points, or a draft follow-up grounded in the actual email and meeting history. At the dashboard level, I can reason across the full pipeline at once. It behaves less like a chatbot and more like an advisor with memory, context, and a point of view.

What I like about the build is that it solves a real workflow problem end to end. It replaced the patchwork of research tools, bookmarks, notes, and one-off prompts with a system that helps me qualify faster, stay organized, and act with better context.

What I figured out along the way:

  • Connected six APIs across research, email, calendar, transcripts, and LLM workflows, each with different auth and data constraints
  • Built the whole product as a Manifest V3 Chrome extension with no backend, keeping the experience fast and local-first
  • Designed a unified data model where companies and opportunities live in the same record, so context stays synced
  • Created contextual AI workflows that use live company data, relationship history, and personal preferences instead of generic prompts

I use it in my actual job search. It replaced my spreadsheets, job-post bookmarks, and a lot of the manual context switching that made the process slower than it needed to be.

How I Work

Operating Principles

Derived from an intrinsic motivators assessment I took a couple of years ago, synthesized and sanity-checked against real working patterns over time.

Powered by Attuned — Reiss Motivation Profile, completed a couple of years ago. Model-assisted synthesis, sanity-checked against real patterns.

  • Collective ownership. I'm motivated by teams that win together — shared outcomes, shared responsibility, low siloing.
  • Signal over activity. I prefer environments where impact is visible and decisions have consequences — not optics or process theater.
  • High standards, low ego. I value rational debate and strong opinions, but I'm aligned to the team goal, not personal credit.
  • Authority matched with accountability. Clear lanes, clear expectations, and accountability without micromanagement.
  • Fast feedback loops. I do best when iteration cycles are short and learning compounds in the open.
  • Upside-oriented, not comfort-oriented. I'll trade predictability for real leverage, as long as the game is worth playing.
